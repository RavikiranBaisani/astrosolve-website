Zodiac Compatibility: Best and Worst Matches by Sign

By Astrologer Ananya 10 min read Kundli Matching · Beginner

Every sign pairing carries a traditional read — some combinations are classically described as easy and natural, others as needing more conscious effort. This guide walks through the Rashi-to-Rashi logic behind those readings, sign by sign, while being honest about how much a "zodiac compatibility" table can and cannot tell you.

"What's your sign, and are we compatible?" is one of the oldest questions in astrology, in any tradition. In the Vedic system, when we talk about zodiac compatibility we are almost always talking about Rashi compatibility — the sign your Moon occupied at birth, not the Sun sign most Western readers grow up with. If you have not already, it's worth reading our guide on Ascendant vs Sun Sign vs Moon Sign to understand why Vedic astrology centres the Moon sign rather than the Sun sign; we won't re-cover that ground here. What follows assumes "your sign" means your Chandra Rashi, and the compatibility logic below is the classical Rashi-to-Rashi framework, which sits conceptually close to — but is calculated quite differently from — the popular Western "sun sign compatibility" many people already know.

The Classical Basis: Elements and Temperament

Vedic astrology groups the 12 Rashis into four elemental families of three signs each. This grouping is one of the oldest lenses used to read compatibility at a glance, because signs sharing an element are thought to share a basic outlook on life.

ElementRashisGeneral temperament
Fire (Agni)Aries, Leo, SagittariusInitiative, drive, directness
Earth (Prithvi)Taurus, Virgo, CapricornPracticality, patience, stability
Air (Vayu)Gemini, Libra, AquariusIdeas, communication, sociability
Water (Jala)Cancer, Scorpio, PiscesFeeling, intuition, depth

The classical tendency runs like this: signs of the same element tend to understand each other's instincts easily, and Fire pairs comfortably with Air (both are active, expressive elements) while Earth pairs comfortably with Water (both are more grounded, feeling-oriented elements). This is one of the oldest reasons an astrologer glances at two Rashis and forms an initial, provisional impression before going any further.

Alongside element, each Rashi also carries a modality — Chara (movable), Sthira (fixed), and Dwiswabhava (dual/mutable) — describing how a sign initiates, sustains, or adapts to change. Two movable signs can both want to lead; two fixed signs can both dig in and refuse to budge; a movable-and-fixed pairing sometimes balances initiation with follow-through. Element tells you about temperament; modality tells you about how two temperaments negotiate change together. Traditional readers weigh both.

The Angular Relationships: Where Extra Care Is Traditionally Advised

Beyond element and modality, classical astrology also looks at the numerical distance between two Rashis counted around the zodiac wheel. Certain angles are traditionally flagged as needing more conscious effort:

  • 6th/8th axis — signs six or eight positions apart from one another. This is traditionally considered one of the more challenging axes, often associated with friction, power struggles, or a sense of working at cross purposes.
  • 2nd/12th axis — signs two or twelve positions apart. This axis is traditionally read as involving a values or resource mismatch — one partner's priorities can feel foreign or draining to the other.

These angular relationships are part of why, for example, Aries and Virgo (a 6/8 relationship) or Taurus and Gemini (a 2/12 relationship) are often listed among the harder pairings for their respective signs below — not because either sign is "worse," but because the geometry between them is classically read as requiring more deliberate bridge-building.

Notice how the "easier" columns generally cluster around same-element and Fire–Air or Earth–Water pairs, while the "needs more effort" columns generally reflect a 6/8 or 2/12 relationship. That consistency is exactly why this framework has persisted for so long as a quick, at-a-glance starting point — it is legible and it is grounded in a real classical logic. It is also exactly why it should stay a starting point.

A sign pairing is a conversation-starter, not a verdict. Two people with a "difficult" sign pairing in this table regularly build strong, lasting marriages once their full charts are read together — and two people with an "easy" pairing can still face real, serious challenges. Rashi is one placement in a chart of dozens. Treat this table as the first question in a longer conversation, never as the final answer.

Why Sign-to-Sign Compatibility Is Only the Entry Point

Here is the honest limitation of everything above: your Rashi is a single data point — the sign the Moon occupied at the moment you were born, out of a full chart that also carries the Ascendant, the other eight planets, the twelve houses, and dozens of finer distinctions within the Moon's own placement. Two people can share the same "hard" Rashi pairing in this table and still be an excellent match once you look at their actual nakshatras, planetary strengths, and house lords. Two people can share an "easy" pairing and still clash badly on the details that a Rashi table cannot see at all.

This is exactly the gap that real Vedic kundli matching is built to close. Traditional Guna Milan, or Ashtakoot Milan, does not stop at "which sign are you" — it compares the bride's and groom's exact birth nakshatras across eight weighted categories (kootas), scoring compatibility out of 36 points. That includes factors like Nadi, the single highest-weighted koota, tied to constitutional type and worth 8 of the 36 points on its own — a level of precision that a 12-sign table simply cannot offer. If you want to see how much more individualized real matching is compared to sign compatibility, our guide to Nadi Dosha in kundli matching walks through exactly how that works, including the well-established conditions under which even a serious-sounding mismatch is traditionally considered cancelled.

Chart matching also looks well beyond the Moon sign alone. Mangal Dosha, for instance, depends on where Mars sits in each partner's individual chart — it has nothing to do with which Rashi pairing you fall into on a table like this one. A full compatibility reading weighs Rashi, nakshatra, Nadi, Mangal Dosha, and several other kootas together, and a good astrologer reads all of it as one connected picture rather than a checklist of pass/fail boxes.

So use the table above the way it is meant to be used: as a light, entry-level read that can spark a useful conversation with a partner, a family member, or an astrologer — not as grounds to rule anyone in or out. The real answer to "are we compatible" almost always requires an actual kundli, not just a sign.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is zodiac compatibility the same as kundli matching?

No. Zodiac (Rashi) compatibility is a broad, sign-level read based on element and angular relationships between two Moon signs. Kundli matching, or Guna Milan, is far more precise — it compares exact birth nakshatras across eight weighted categories worth 36 points total, and factors in placements like Mangal Dosha that a sign table cannot capture at all.

Should I use my Sun sign or Moon sign for this table?

In the Vedic tradition, use your Moon sign (Rashi). Most Western zodiac compatibility content is built around the Sun sign, but Vedic compatibility reading is centred on the Chandra Rashi. See our guide on Ascendant vs Sun Sign vs Moon Sign if you are unsure which one you actually are.

Does a "difficult" sign pairing mean a marriage won't work?

No. A challenging Rashi-to-Rashi angle is a traditional flag to look more closely, not a prediction of failure. Many couples with classically "harder" sign pairings have strong, long marriages once the fuller chart — nakshatra, Nadi, planetary strengths — is read and shows good support.

Can two people of the same Rashi be a good match?

Often, yes — sharing a Rashi usually means sharing an element and a similar emotional temperament, which is frequently read as comfortable. But sameness is not automatic compatibility either; a full match still looks at each person's individual nakshatra, planetary lords, and other kootas rather than assuming a same-sign pairing is automatically ideal.
