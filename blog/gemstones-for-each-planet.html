Gemstones for Each Planet: A Complete Guide

By Astrologer Ananya 11 min read Vedic Astrology · Beginner

In Vedic astrology, a gemstone is not chosen for its beauty or your birth month — it is chosen to strengthen one specific planet in your chart. The nine grahas each have a gemstone, together known as the Navaratna, and knowing which one is right for you depends entirely on your own horoscope, not on a calendar.

Gemstone remedies are among the oldest and most trusted tools in the astrologer's kit. The idea is elegant: each of the nine grahas is said to resonate with a particular gemstone, and wearing that gem — correctly set, cut, and worn — is believed to amplify the planet's positive influence in your life. This guide walks through all nine stones, explains the principle that makes a gemstone "right" for you, and, just as importantly, sets out the cautions every serious tradition insists on.

The Navaratna: Nine Gems for Nine Planets

Navaratna is Sanskrit for "nine gems." It refers to the set of nine gemstones, each mapped to one of the nine grahas of Vedic astrology — the seven visible planets plus the two lunar nodes, Rahu and Ketu. A Navaratna piece of jewellery combines all nine into a single ornament, arranged in a traditional pattern with the Sun's ruby at the centre. But in day-to-day practice, an astrologer far more often prescribes a single stone for a single planet, rather than the full set.

Classical texts group the gems into Maharatnas (the "great gems" — ruby, pearl, red coral, emerald, yellow sapphire, diamond and blue sapphire) and treat hessonite and cat's eye alongside them as the nodal stones. Here is how each graha maps to its primary gemstone.

Sun — Ruby (Manikya)

The ruby is the Sun's stone, and like the Sun it is bold. It is worn to reinforce a Sun that governs the self, confidence, and standing in the world. Traditionally set in gold and worn on the ring finger, it is associated with leadership, vitality and a strong sense of purpose.

Moon — Pearl (Moti)

The pearl carries the Moon's cool, receptive nature. Because the Moon rules the mind and emotions in Vedic astrology, the pearl is prescribed to steady an unsettled mind, ease anxiety, and support emotional balance. It is one of the gentler stones and is usually set in silver.

Mars — Red Coral (Moonga)

Red coral is Mars's gem, worn to strengthen courage, physical energy and the will to act. It appears frequently in the context of Mangal Dosha discussions, since Mars is the planet at the heart of that affliction — though whether coral helps in any given chart depends entirely on Mars's role there.

Mercury — Emerald (Panna)

The emerald belongs to Mercury, the planet of intellect, speech and commerce. It is worn to sharpen communication, memory and analytical thinking, and is a popular choice for those in study, writing, trade or negotiation — again, only where Mercury is favourable.

Jupiter — Yellow Sapphire (Pukhraj)

Yellow sapphire is Jupiter's stone and one of the most widely recommended gems, because Jupiter is the great benefic of Vedic astrology. It is associated with wisdom, prosperity, higher learning and good fortune, and is traditionally set in gold.

Venus — Diamond (Heera)

The diamond corresponds to Venus, the planet of love, beauty, art and comfort. It is worn to enhance relationships, refinement and material ease. Because a fine astrological-grade diamond is costly, substitutes are common here — more on those below.

Saturn — Blue Sapphire (Neelam)

Blue sapphire is Saturn's gem, prized for discipline, focus, endurance and career progress. It is also the most talked-about stone in this list — and the one that demands the most caution, for reasons we come to shortly.

Rahu — Hessonite (Gomed)

Hessonite, a honey-to-brown garnet, is the stone of Rahu, the Moon's north node. It is worn to calm the confusion and restlessness associated with a difficult Rahu, and to support clarity, protection and worldly ambition.

Ketu — Cat's Eye (Lehsunia)

Cat's eye, with its distinctive shifting band of light, is Ketu's gem. Ketu is the planet of detachment and spirituality, and the stone is traditionally linked to insight, protection from sudden reversals, and progress on the inner path.

The Guiding Principle: Your Chart, Not Your Birth Month

This is the single most important thing to understand, and it is where Vedic gemstones differ sharply from Western "birthstones." A Western birthstone is assigned by the calendar month you were born in — a fixed, one-size-fits-all list. A Vedic gemstone is nothing of the kind. It is selected to strengthen a particular planet as that planet sits in your individual birth chart.

That means two people born on the very same day can be advised entirely different stones, because their charts are cast for different times and places and their planets sit in different houses with different strengths. The astrologer studies which planets rule the favourable areas of your chart, which are well-placed, and which need reinforcing — and prescribes the gem accordingly. There is no shortcut to this, and certainly no "wear the stone for your sign" rule that holds up in classical practice.

Read this before you buy anything. A gemstone must be selected by a qualified astrologer for your specific chart. Worn blindly, the wrong stone can strengthen an unfavourable planet and make matters worse rather than better. Blue Sapphire (Neelam) deserves special mention: it is famously the fastest-acting of all the gems, and its effects — good or bad — are said to show within days, which is why it is traditionally trialled cautiously before being worn permanently. Never self-prescribe a gemstone on the strength of an online list, this one included.

Substitutes (Upratna): The Affordable Alternatives

The primary gems can be expensive, and a genuine astrological-quality ruby, diamond or blue sapphire is beyond many budgets. Classical tradition anticipated this with the upratna system — secondary or substitute stones that are said to carry a similar planetary vibration in a milder, cheaper form. A white sapphire or white zircon, for instance, is a common stand-in for the diamond as a Venus stone; opal is another Venus substitute. Substitutes are also chosen when the primary stone would be too intense for a chart.

The catch is that a substitute is still a planetary remedy, so the same rule applies: which upratna suits you, and whether one is appropriate at all, is a decision for your astrologer, not a way to shop by price alone.

How Gemstones Are Worn: Where Traditions Differ

Beyond the choice of stone, the practical details of wearing it — the metal it is set in, the finger it goes on, the weight in carats, and the day and time it is first worn — are all part of the prescription. And here traditions and lineages vary. Some astrologers are strict about setting the Sun's ruby only in gold and the Moon's pearl only in silver; others allow more flexibility. Weight is often calculated in relation to the wearer, and the first wearing is commonly timed to the weekday and hour of the ruling planet, after cleansing and a brief prayer.

None of this is universal, and reputable astrologers will differ on the specifics. If you are given a gemstone remedy, follow the instructions of the astrologer who read your chart rather than mixing rules from several sources. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear a gemstone based on my zodiac sign?

Not reliably. "Wear this stone for your sign" is a Western-style shortcut that classical Vedic practice does not endorse. Your gemstone is chosen from your full birth chart — the placement and strength of the planets, and which ones rule the favourable parts of your horoscope — not from your sign or birth month alone.

Is Blue Sapphire dangerous?

Blue Sapphire (Neelam) is not dangerous in itself, but it is powerful and fast-acting, so it is treated with more care than any other gem. Because its effects are said to appear within days, tradition recommends a trial period before it is worn permanently, and only ever on the advice of an astrologer who has studied your chart. It should never be worn on impulse.

Do gemstones really work?

Gemstone remedies are a well-established part of Vedic tradition, and many people wear them with conviction. As an astrologer, my honest guidance is to treat a gemstone as one supportive tool among many, chosen carefully for your chart — not as a guaranteed fix for health, money, or relationships. No stone replaces professional medical, legal, or financial advice.

How do I know which gemstone suits me?

The only sound way is to have your birth chart analysed by a qualified astrologer, who will look at which planets are favourable and need strengthening and then recommend the stone, weight, metal, finger, and timing to match. Any list — this one included — can teach you the principles, but it cannot tell you your stone.
