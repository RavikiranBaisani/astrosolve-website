Mangal Dosha Remedies: Effective Solutions Explained

By Astrologer Ananya 9 min read Vedic Astrology · Beginner

Mangal Dosha remedies are traditional practices meant to soften the harsh side of Mars in a birth chart — not switches that turn a placement off. The ones worth doing are chosen for the individual chart, undertaken with the right intention, and understood for what they are: supportive customs, not guaranteed fixes for marriage, temperament, or fate.

Few phrases cause as much worry at the matchmaking stage as "the chart is Manglik." That worry is usually out of proportion to what the placement actually means — but it does send many families looking for remedies. This guide walks through the traditional solutions astrologers cite for Mangal Dosha, explains where different lineages disagree, and offers a calmer way to think about the whole subject.

A Quick Recap: What Mangal Dosha Is

Mangal Dosha (also called Manglik Dosha or Kuja Dosha) is said to arise when Mars — the fiery, assertive graha of energy and drive — sits in certain houses of the birth chart, classically the 1st, 4th, 7th, 8th, or 12th from the Lagna, and in some traditions also from the Moon or Venus. Because several of these houses touch marriage and domestic life, the placement is examined most closely at the time of matchmaking. If you are not yet sure whether a chart carries it or how it is judged, start here: What Is Mangal Dosha, and how to check for it. This article assumes you already know the placement is present and want to understand the remedies.

Before Any Remedy: Judge the Severity

The single most useful "remedy" is an honest reading of how strong the dosha actually is. Classical Parashari astrology lists numerous cancellation conditions — combinations in which the dosha is considered nullified or greatly reduced. Mars in its own sign or exaltation, certain aspects from benefics, a matching placement in the partner's chart, and specific house positions can all lighten or cancel it. Age is often cited too: many traditions hold that Mars's edge mellows over time.

This matters because a chart judged "Manglik" on a checklist may carry little practical weight once the whole Kundli is read. A remedy for a problem that isn't really there is wasted effort — and sometimes a source of needless anxiety. A qualified astrologer should first tell you how much the dosha counts before recommending anything at all.

1. Devotional — Worship, Mantra, and Fasting

The most widely recommended remedies are devotional, because they ask nothing of your chart and carry no risk of being "wrong" for you. Mars is traditionally linked with Lord Hanuman and with Kartikeya, so worship of Hanuman is the go-to practice: reciting the Hanuman Chalisa, visiting a Hanuman temple, and offering red flowers or sindoor, particularly on Tuesdays. Some astrologers add the Mangal Beej Mantra ("Om Kraam Kreem Kraum Sah Bhaumaya Namah") or the Navagraha invocation to Mangal, chanted a fixed number of times.

Fasting on Tuesday — Mars's own weekday — is the companion practice, often kept from sunrise until an evening meal, sometimes with a preference for red or wheat-based food and avoidance of salt, depending on family custom. The point is less the food rule than the discipline: turning a fiery day toward restraint and devotion. Details vary widely between households and lineages, so follow the form your own tradition or astrologer gives rather than treating any one version as the rule.

2. Ritual — Kumbh Vivah

Kumbh Vivah is the best-known Mangal Dosha ritual, and also the most misunderstood. The Manglik person is symbolically "married" first — to a peepal or banana tree, to a clay pot (kumbh), or to an idol of Vishnu — on the idea that this first union absorbs the dosha's effect, so the real marriage that follows is spared it. Regional variants abound: some communities marry to a tree, others to a pot that is then broken or floated away, others to a Vishnu idol; the object, the ceremony, and even whether it is recommended at all shift from region to region and family to family.

Because it is elaborate and public, Kumbh Vivah is usually reserved for cases an astrologer judges strong — not something to arrange on the strength of a checklist alone. If a lighter reading of the chart shows the dosha is mild or cancelled, most astrologers would not ask for it.

3. Charitable — Donation and Seva

Charity redirects Mars's outward, assertive energy into giving. The classic donations are red items associated with Mars: masoor dal (red lentils), red cloth, jaggery, copper, and red flowers, offered on Tuesdays or given to those in need. Seva — service without expectation, such as feeding people or helping at a temple — is valued in the same spirit. These practices are low-risk and, at worst, do some good in the world regardless of astrology.

4. Gemstone — Red Coral (Moonga)

Red coral, or Moonga, is the gemstone of Mars, and it is frequently mentioned in the same breath as Mangal Dosha — but here the advice needs real care. A gemstone strengthens its planet; it does not tranquillise it. Worn on the wrong chart, a stone meant to empower Mars could amplify exactly the traits you were hoping to soften. For that reason, coral should only ever be worn after a proper, chart-specific analysis by an astrologer, with attention to Mars's dignity, house, and role for your particular Lagna — never as a default "Manglik remedy" bought off a shelf.

It is also worth knowing that traditions genuinely differ here. Plenty of respected astrologers are cautious about gemstones as a first resort and prefer to lean on devotional and charitable remedies, reserving stones for specific, well-justified cases. If one astrologer prescribes coral and another declines to, that is not a contradiction so much as two schools of practice. Treat any gemstone recommendation as a decision to make with an astrologer, not a purchase to make alone.

5. Matching — Marrying Another Manglik

The oldest "remedy" of all is the one built into matchmaking itself: pairing a Manglik with another Manglik. The traditional reasoning is that when both partners carry the placement, its effects are considered to offset or balance between them, so neither is disadvantaged. This is why the question at the matching stage is rarely "is this person Manglik?" in isolation, but "how do the two charts sit together?" — a comparison best done by an astrologer reading both Kundlis rather than by matching a single label.

The one caveat that matters most: remedies for Mangal Dosha should be personalised to the actual chart, not self-prescribed from a list. Gemstones in particular can do more harm than good on the wrong chart and should never be worn without individual analysis. These are traditional, supportive practices undertaken in good faith — they carry no guarantee of any specific outcome in marriage, health, or life, and no astrologer can honestly promise one.

A Balanced View: The Dosha Is Often Overstated

It helps to say plainly what a responsible astrologer will: Mangal Dosha is one of the most over-dramatised ideas in popular astrology. Its severity varies enormously from chart to chart, the classical cancellation conditions mean many "Manglik" charts carry little practical weight, and Mars in a strong, well-placed position can be an asset — courage, drive, and protectiveness — rather than a flaw to be fixed. Fear is not a good reason to perform a ritual, and no one should enter marriage feeling defective over a single placement.

The healthiest approach is order of operations: read the whole chart, judge whether the dosha genuinely counts, and only then consider remedies — starting with the gentle devotional and charitable ones, and treating gemstones and elaborate rituals as considered decisions, not reflexes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Mangal Dosha remedies really work?

They are traditional supportive practices, not guaranteed cures, and no honest astrologer will promise a specific result from them. Many people find real value in the discipline and intention behind fasting, worship, and charity. What these remedies cannot do is override a full reading of the chart — which is why judging whether the dosha even counts comes first.

What is Kumbh Vivah?

It is a symbolic marriage performed for a Manglik person before their actual wedding — to a peepal or banana tree, a clay pot, or a Vishnu idol — on the belief that this first union absorbs the dosha so the real marriage is spared it. The exact object and ritual vary by region and family, and it is usually reserved for cases an astrologer judges strong.

Should I wear red coral for Mangal Dosha?

Not by default. Red coral (Moonga) strengthens Mars rather than calming it, so on the wrong chart it can worsen the very traits you wanted to soften. Wear it only if an astrologer recommends it after analysing your specific chart — and know that some astrologers prefer to avoid gemstones as a first resort altogether.

Can remedies fully remove Mangal Dosha?

A placement in the birth chart is permanent — remedies are traditionally understood to soften its influence, not erase it. In practice, the classical cancellation conditions and the overall strength of the chart often matter more than any remedy, and a mild or cancelled dosha may need nothing done at all.
