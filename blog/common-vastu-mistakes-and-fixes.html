Common Vastu Mistakes and How to Fix Them

By Astrologer Ananya 9 min read Vastu · Beginner

Most homes are not built to a Vastu chart from the ground up, and that is fine — Vastu Shastra was never meant to be a pass/fail exam that condemns an entire house over one flaw. It is a set of directional principles for balancing the five elements within a living space, and nearly every common mistake has a workable correction that does not involve breaking a single wall.

The classical texts on Vastu Shastra treat a home the way Parashari astrology treats a birth chart: as a field of forces that can be read, understood, and gently adjusted rather than feared. Much like the simple daily remedies used in Jyotish, most Vastu corrections are small, repeatable habits rather than one-time overhauls. What follows is a working list of the mistakes that show up most often in ordinary homes — apartments, rented flats, older houses inherited as-is — along with the remedy each tradition offers for correcting it without demolition.

The Main Entrance

The main door is treated as the primary point through which energy, opportunity, and the household's fortune enter the home, so its condition matters more than its exact direction. A dim, blocked, squeaky, or cluttered entrance dampens whatever the direction itself promises. A broken doorbell, a door that sticks, or a foot mat piled with old footwear all count as small entrance faults that add up.

The fix: keep the entrance well lit, clean, and unobstructed — no shoe racks, bicycles, or bins pressed right against the door. A fresh coat of paint, a working bell, and a threshold that opens fully (not scraping or jammed) go a long way. If the door genuinely faces a difficult direction such as south-west and cannot be moved, a bright light near the entrance and a Vastu pyramid or small Swastik motif on the outer wall are the traditional non-structural corrections, though schools differ on how much weight to give symbolic fixes versus lighting and cleanliness alone.

Rooms in the Wrong Zone

Vastu divides a plot into eight directions plus the center, and assigns each an element and a purpose. Three placement errors recur constantly.

Bedroom in the Northeast

The Northeast (Ishan) corner is associated with water and meditative, contemplative energy — good for a puja room or a study, not for the heavier, more active energy a bedroom carries. A master bedroom here is often linked in traditional readings to restlessness or shallow sleep, though most texts point to the southwest as the ideal for the primary bedroom instead.

The fix: if moving bedrooms is not possible, keep the Northeast bedroom sparse and light — pale colors, minimal furniture, no heavy almirah, and an early bedtime routine to work with the corner's calmer energy rather than against it.

Kitchen in the Northeast

This is considered one of the more serious placement errors because it sets fire (the kitchen's element) directly against water (the Northeast's element) — two forces the tradition treats as naturally opposed. A kitchen here is often connected to friction in the household and, in some traditional readings, to the kind of financial strain covered in our guide to remedies for career and money obstacles. The Southeast (Agni corner) is the classical ideal for a kitchen instead.

The fix: a full kitchen relocation is rarely realistic in a finished home, so the practical correction is to move the stove itself — the actual cooking flame — to the southeast corner of the kitchen if at all possible, so the cook faces east while cooking. A small copper or brass item and consistent cleanliness at that corner are the supporting touches most Vastu consultants add.

Toilet in the Center (Brahmasthan)

The center of the home, the Brahmasthan, is meant to stay open, light, and unburdened — it is treated as the point where all eight directions meet and balance each other. A toilet placed here is considered one of the most disruptive faults a floor plan can carry, since it introduces waste and moisture at the one point meant to stay clear.

The fix: keep the toilet lid closed at all times, the door shut when not in use, and the space well ventilated with an exhaust fan running. A small bowl of rock salt in the corner (changed weekly) is a traditional absorption remedy. Where the layout allows, some consultants also recommend a symbolic Vastu correction such as a copper coin buried at the room's threshold — a practice not universally prescribed, so treat it as optional rather than essential.

A note on scope: this article is a general roundup across a typical floor plan rather than a deep dive into any single zone. If a particular placement issue is causing ongoing friction at home, it can help to look at it alongside chart-based remedies, such as those in our guide to Mangal Dosha remedies, since Vastu and planetary influences are often read together in practice.

Heavy Furniture and Beams in the Wrong Zones

Structural weight is meant to sit in the Southwest, the zone associated with stability and the earth element — heavy wardrobes, safes, and storage units belong there. The same weight placed in the Northeast or center works against those zones' lighter, more open character, and an exposed overhead beam running above a bed or a work desk is considered to press down on the person beneath it, disturbing rest or focus.

The fix: without moving walls, simply relocate heavy furniture toward the southwest wall of each room over time. For an unavoidable beam over a bed or desk, shifting the bed or desk a few feet so the beam no longer runs directly overhead is the simplest correction; where that is not possible, a false ceiling panel or a pair of small wooden flutes hung from the beam (a common symbolic remedy) are said to soften its effect.

Mirror Misplacement

Mirrors are treated as active, energy-doubling objects in Vastu, which makes their placement more consequential than it looks. A mirror facing the bed is widely discouraged, since it is believed to double restlessness and strain in the relationship it reflects. A mirror directly opposite the main door is thought to reflect incoming fortune straight back out, and one facing a toilet or a cluttered store room doubles exactly the wrong kind of energy.

The fix: mirrors are best placed on north or east walls, angled away from the bed and never directly opposite the entrance door. If a mirror cannot be moved — built into a wardrobe door facing the bed, for instance — a cloth cover used at night, or simply reorienting the wardrobe door so it stays closed while sleeping, resolves the issue without any carpentry.

Water Element Mistakes

Water is one of the five elements Vastu balances most carefully, and two water-related errors are especially common in urban homes. An overhead water tank placed in the Northeast puts weight and water pressure exactly where the corner is meant to stay light and open, which traditional texts connect to family discord and blocked opportunity. Underground water storage or a borewell dug in the Southeast or Southwest similarly puts water where fire and earth are meant to dominate, unsettling the balance those zones are supposed to hold.

The fix: the classical placement for both overhead tanks and underground water storage is the Northeast for a sump/underground tank and the ideal loft position; where an existing structure has it reversed, most consultants suggest keeping the Northeast area beneath or around a misplaced tank clutter-free and well lit as a working compromise rather than relocating the tank itself, which is rarely practical after construction.

Clutter and Its Energetic Significance

Clutter is not treated as a cosmetic issue in Vastu — it is read as stagnant energy that blocks the free circulation the tradition calls Vastu Purusha's breath through the home. A cluttered Northeast, in particular, is considered to choke off clarity and growth, while junk piled behind doors slows the very energy the entrance is meant to draw in.

The fix: a periodic clear-out — quarterly rather than only at festival time — of the entrance, the Northeast corner, and under-stair storage does more for a home's Vastu than most symbolic remedies combined. Keep walkways and corners genuinely walkable, not just visually tidy.

Broken Items and Non-Functional Clocks

A broken clock, a cracked mirror, a wall clock that has stopped, or appliances kept "for spares" are all treated the same way in Vastu: as dead, non-circulating energy sitting inside a living space. A stopped clock in particular is considered to symbolically freeze the flow of time and opportunity in the room it hangs in.

The fix: repair or discard broken clocks, mirrors, and appliances rather than storing them indefinitely. This is the least mystical of all the fixes on this list and arguably the most reliably useful one — a home genuinely does feel different once nothing broken is quietly sitting in it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to break walls to fix a Vastu dosha?

Rarely, for the everyday mistakes covered here. Most traditional remedies work with what already exists — repositioning furniture, adjusting the stove's placement within an existing kitchen, using mirrors, colors, and symbolic items, and simply keeping key zones clean and open. Structural correction is reserved for severe defects, and even then most consultants exhaust the non-structural options first.

Can Vastu remedies undo a difficult birth chart?

Vastu and Jyotish are related but distinct disciplines. Vastu balances the energy of a physical space; a birth chart reads planetary influence on a person's life. Neither is a substitute for the other, and Vastu should not be treated as a guaranteed fix for problems that a chart reading, a doctor, or a financial advisor would be better placed to address.

Is one wrongly placed room enough to ruin a home's Vastu?

No single flaw is usually treated as decisive on its own. Vastu evaluates a home as a whole — one misplaced toilet or bedroom is a factor to correct, not a verdict on the entire house. This is also why remedies exist in the first place: to bring balance back to an imperfect layout rather than demanding a rebuild.

Do all Vastu consultants agree on these rules?

Not entirely. Regional schools and individual consultants sometimes differ on finer points — how strictly to weigh a tank's exact corner, whether a particular symbolic remedy is essential or optional, or how much to prioritize the entrance direction versus its condition. The broad principles above (elements, the Brahmasthan, avoiding clutter and disrepair) are consistent across most traditional texts even where details vary.
