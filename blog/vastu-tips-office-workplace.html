Vastu Tips for Office and Workplace Success

By Astrologer Ananya 8 min read Vastu · Beginner

Vastu Shastra treats a workplace as a living arrangement of direction and energy, not just square footage. The tradition holds that where you sit, which way you face, and where money and paperwork are stored can support — or quietly work against — the effort you put into your business. None of this replaces good strategy or hard work; it is a supporting practice, layered on top of them.

Vastu Shastra is the classical Indian science of architecture and space, built on the idea that the eight directions each carry a different quality of energy, governed by a presiding deity or planetary force. Applied to a home, it shapes where the kitchen or the front door should sit. Applied to an office, the same logic is used to decide where the owner sits, where money is kept, and how staff are arranged around them. This piece walks through the main recommendations as they are commonly taught, along with the reasoning behind them and what to do when your office layout does not cooperate.

Location and Direction of the Office Within a Building

Traditional texts favor a plot or unit in the north, east, or northeast portion of a larger building for a business, since these directions are associated with the Sun, Mercury, and Jupiter — energies linked to growth, communication, and clarity of thought. The main entrance is generally preferred on the north, east, or northeast wall for the same reason: it is thought to let opportunity and income enter the space more easily.

The southwest is treated differently. It is associated with stability and weight rather than movement, so it is considered better suited to storage, the owner's cabin, or heavier fixed furniture than to the entrance or the reception area. A northwest-facing office is often linked to businesses built on networking, sales, or public dealing, since that direction is associated with movement and change.

Desk and Seating Direction for Owners and Employees

The most repeated instruction in office Vastu is about which way you face while working, not which way the desk itself points. East is generally recommended for the business owner or decision-maker, since it is ruled by the Sun and is associated with authority, visibility, and leadership. North is the second commonly recommended direction, linked to Kubera, the traditional keeper of wealth, and is often suggested for anyone whose work touches money directly — finance, accounts, trading.

South-facing seating is the direction most consistently discouraged for a working desk, on the reasoning that it can bring restlessness or short temper into decision-making. West is considered workable, though secondary to north and east, and is sometimes used for support functions rather than leadership roles.

Why Sitting With Your Back to the Door Is Discouraged

A second, closely related rule is about what is behind the chair, not just what is in front of it. Vastu practitioners generally advise against a desk position where a person's back faces the main door or an open walkway. The idea is that a solid wall behind the chair offers a sense of support and stability, while an open door or passage behind you is thought to create an undercurrent of alertness or unease that can affect focus and confidence over the workday. For the same reason, sitting directly facing the entrance — so that every visitor walks in and stares straight at you — is also generally avoided; a position slightly off-axis from the door is preferred, so you can see who enters without your desk being what they see first.

Cash Box, Locker, and Accounts Placement

The cash locker, safe, or accounts area is one of the more specific placements in office Vastu. The southwest corner is the direction most commonly recommended for a locker or safe, on the logic that southwest carries a stabilizing, retentive quality — useful for something you want to hold onto rather than let flow away. Some schools instead recommend the south wall specifically for a cash box, provided it opens toward the north, so that when the locker is unlocked, money symbolically "faces" the direction of wealth.

Whichever exact corner is followed, the shared principle across sources is consistent: the locker should not be visible from the main entrance, should not have its opening face south, and should be placed against a solid wall rather than a glass partition.

Boss's Cabin vs. Staff Seating

The owner's or manager's cabin is typically placed in the southwest part of the office, the same zone associated with stability and authority, while staff desks are arranged in the north, east, or center of the space, closer to the flow of daylight and entry. This mirrors a broader Vastu pattern seen in homes too, where the heaviest, most senior placement sits toward the southwest and lighter, more active zones sit toward the north and east.

Within the cabin itself, the same seating rules apply at a larger scale: the boss should ideally face east or north, with a solid wall behind the chair, and the desk positioned so the door is visible without sitting directly in line with it. A cabin placed at the very back of the office, away from the entrance, is often preferred so authority is not undermined by too much foot traffic passing directly by.

Plants and Symbols Commonly Used

The money plant is the single most common addition recommended for offices in this tradition, usually placed in the southeast corner — the direction associated with Venus and with the flow of finances — and often kept climbing upward rather than trailing down, on the belief that upward growth mirrors upward fortune. Bamboo stalks (commonly arranged in sets of three, five, or seven) are another frequent choice, tied to steady, resilient growth.

Beyond plants, a small number of symbols recur across offices that follow Vastu: a Kuber yantra or a Swastik near the cash locker, a Laughing Buddha near the entrance for a welcoming energy, and a Northeast-placed water feature or fountain, thought to support the flow of opportunity into the space. None of these are treated as substitutes for sound business decisions — they are framed as supporting elements layered onto ordinary hard work.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

A few missteps come up again and again in office Vastu guidance:

  • Clutter near the entrance or under desks. Piled files, old furniture, or overflowing bins near the main door are thought to block the same energy the entrance is meant to invite in.
  • A desk placed directly opposite the entrance. Sitting in a straight line with the main door, so that every person entering looks straight down at you, is considered draining rather than commanding.
  • Blocked or narrow walking pathways. Furniture or storage that forces staff to squeeze past each other is thought to trap stagnant energy in corners rather than letting it circulate.
  • Overhead beams above a desk or chair. Sitting directly under an exposed structural beam is generally avoided, as it is believed to create a subtle sense of pressure over the person seated beneath it.
  • Mirrors facing the main door. A mirror positioned to reflect the entrance directly back is thought to send incoming opportunity straight back out again.
A quick gut check: stand at your office's main entrance and look in. If the first thing you see is clutter, a mirror throwing the doorway back at you, or someone sitting with their back turned squarely to you, those are usually the first three fixes worth making before anything else.

Remedies for a Non-Ideal Layout

Most rented offices were never designed with Vastu in mind, and moving walls or entrances is rarely practical. The tradition offers lighter fixes for exactly this situation:

  • Can't avoid a south-facing desk? Some practitioners suggest a small mirror or a reflective surface angled so the person can glimpse the door without turning fully around, easing the discomfort of an unavoidable seating direction.
  • Entrance not in the north or east? A small Vastu pyramid, a Swastik symbol, or a bright light fixed above the existing door is a commonly suggested way to soften a less-favored entrance direction without rebuilding it.
  • Locker stuck in an inconvenient corner? Keeping it closed, against a solid wall, and out of direct line of sight from the entrance addresses most of the underlying concern even if the exact corner isn't the textbook southwest.
  • Open-plan office with no real "cabin" zone? A partition, a plant, or simply a change in desk orientation for the senior-most person can approximate the southwest-cabin, north-east-staff pattern without construction.

Schools of Vastu differ on how strict these placements need to be — some treat the directional rules as firm, others as a spectrum of "better and worse" rather than "correct and wrong." Where sources disagree, the safer approach is to apply the two or three changes that are actually feasible in your space, rather than trying to force an exact match to a diagram that assumes a building you don't have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Vastu guarantee business success?

No tradition of Vastu claims that alone. It is presented as a supporting practice that arranges a workspace toward stability and clarity — not a substitute for a sound business plan, capital, or effort. Treat it as one input among many, not a promise of an outcome.

What if my desk absolutely must face south?

This is common in real offices where the layout is fixed. The usual workaround is a small angled mirror that lets you see the door without turning your back on it, plus keeping the wall behind your chair solid rather than glass. It is treated as a mitigation, not a full fix.

Where exactly should the money plant go?

Southeast is the direction most commonly recommended, since it is associated with Venus and financial flow. Some practitioners also accept east as a secondary option. Avoid placing it in the northeast, which is generally reserved for open, uncluttered space.

Is there one single correct Vastu layout for every office?

No. Different schools of Vastu Shastra place slightly different weight on directions like southwest for the cabin versus south for the locker. The core principles — favor north/east for people and entrances, southwest for weight and storage, keep pathways clear — are broadly shared, but the finer details vary by tradition and by practitioner.
