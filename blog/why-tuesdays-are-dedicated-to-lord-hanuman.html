Why Tuesdays Are Dedicated to Lord Hanuman

By Astrologer Ananya 8 min read Hindu Mythology · Beginner

Walk past almost any Hanuman temple on a Tuesday morning and you'll find it busier than any other day of the week — queues for darshan, the smell of sindoor and oil, the Hanuman Chalisa playing on a loop somewhere nearby. And yet Hanuman isn't a planet. He has no seat among the Navagraha, the nine grahas that anchor the entire framework of Vedic astrology. So why has popular practice tied him so firmly to one particular weekday, the way it ties the Sun to Sunday or the Moon to Monday?

It's a fair question, and a genuinely interesting one, because the answer isn't a single clean fact so much as a handful of traditions that reinforced each other over centuries until Tuesday-for-Hanuman became one of the most widely recognised devotional pairings in Hindu practice. This article walks through the main explanations you'll hear — some astrological, some mythological, some purely devotional — and is honest about where they're settled tradition and where they're regional variation.

The Puzzle: Hanuman Isn't a Graha

In Vedic astrology, the day-planet system is built entirely around the Navagraha — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, plus the lunar nodes Rahu and Ketu. Each weekday is ruled by one of the seven visible grahas, which is why our guide to which colour to wear each day can map every day of the week to a planet and a colour without any gap. Hanuman, a deity rather than a planetary body, sits outside that system entirely.

And yet in everyday practice, "Tuesday" and "Hanuman" are almost synonymous for millions of devotees. That gap — between the formal astrological system and the lived devotional calendar — is exactly what makes this worth unpacking rather than just accepting as given.

The Mars Connection: Shared Energy, Not Shared Category

The most commonly cited explanation is that Tuesday, or Mangalvar, is ruled by Mars (Mangal) — the graha of courage, physical strength, willpower, and combat. Hanuman, quite independently of any planetary system, is understood in the epics as the supreme embodiment of exactly those qualities: immense physical power, fearless courage, unwavering discipline, and a warrior's readiness to act, all held in service of duty rather than ego.

Because Hanuman's core character so closely mirrors what Mars represents, popular tradition drew the two together even though he was never meant to occupy a planetary slot. He isn't "assigned" to Tuesday the way Mars is; he's associated with it because his energy is read as resonant with the day's ruling planet. It's a pairing built on shared temperament, not shared category — which is worth remembering the next time someone treats it as a hard astrological rule rather than a devotional convention.

Hanuman and the Mangal Dosha Connection

That shared Mars energy has a very practical downstream effect: Hanuman worship on Tuesdays became one of the most popular folk remedies for people affected by Mangal Dosha, the chart condition tied to an afflicted or poorly placed Mars. The logic follows naturally from the pairing above — if Hanuman embodies Mars's better qualities (disciplined strength, courage channeled toward good) rather than its harsher ones (aggression, impulsiveness, conflict), then invoking him on Mars's own day is seen as a way to draw out the constructive side of that planetary energy.

Reciting the Hanuman Chalisa, visiting a temple, and other Tuesday practices show up again and again in remedy lists for Mangal Dosha, alongside fasting and other observances. We've covered that ground in detail in our guide to Mangal Dosha remedies, so this article won't repeat that list — the point here is simply that this remedy tradition is a natural extension of the mythological pairing, not its origin. The devotional connection between Hanuman and Tuesday came first, in spirit; the remedy practice grew out of it.

A Birth on a Tuesday? Tradition Varies

A second explanation you'll often hear is more direct: that Hanuman was born on a Tuesday, and Hanuman Jayanti, his birth anniversary, falls on a date associated with Tuesday in certain regional calendars. This is a real and widely cited tradition, but it isn't a single settled fact across the country. Hanuman Jayanti itself is celebrated on different tithis in different regions — some traditions mark it in the month of Chaitra, others in Kartika, and the specific weekday association varies with the local calendar used to calculate the date in a given year and region.

It's best treated as one contributing thread among several rather than the single, definitive reason for the Tuesday association — a tradition that reinforces the Mars-based logic rather than one that stands entirely on its own.

Sankata Mochan: Reliever of Troubles

The final piece is simply Hanuman's broader devotional character. He is worshipped across India as Sankata Mochan — the reliever of troubles — a protector against fear, negative influences, and obstacles of every kind. That energetic, decisive, protective nature harmonises naturally with a day already understood as governed by assertive, action-oriented Mars. Where Mars can tip toward aggression or conflict if poorly supported, Hanuman's presence is invoked as a steadying, protective counterbalance on the very day that energy runs highest.

This is also why many devotees consider Tuesday an auspicious day to begin undertakings that require courage or carry some risk — a new venture, a difficult conversation, a bold decision — seeking Hanuman's blessing on a day whose energy already favours bold action.

How Tuesday Devotion Is Typically Practiced

Across regions, a fairly consistent set of practices has emerged around Hanuman on Tuesdays. Reciting the Hanuman Chalisa is probably the single most common — many households treat it as a near-daily habit that intensifies specifically on Tuesdays. Temple visits spike noticeably, often with an offering of sindoor (vermillion) and oil applied to the murti, a practice tied to a well-known legend of Hanuman covering himself in sindoor after learning it symbolised long life for Sita's sake. Wearing red ties back to the same Mars-day colour logic covered in our day-wise colour guide, and some devotees observe a partial or full fast through the day as an added discipline.

None of these practices are mandatory in any formal sense — they're customs that have accumulated meaning through repetition and shared belief, which is exactly what gives them their staying power.

This is common practice, not a universal rule. Regional and sectarian variation here is real and significant. Not every tradition ties Hanuman specifically to Tuesday in the same way — many devotees, particularly where Hanuman worship overlaps with easing Saturn's (Shani's) harsher effects, observe Saturday as their primary Hanuman day instead. What this article describes is the dominant popular practice across much of North and Central India, not a single fixed rule that applies identically everywhere. And ultimately, devotional practice is a matter of personal faith and family or regional tradition — nothing here should be read as suggesting that skipping Tuesday specifically carries any negative consequence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Hanuman one of the nine planets (Navagraha)?

No. The Navagraha are the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, and Ketu — Hanuman isn't among them. His strong association with Tuesday comes from shared qualities with Mars (courage, strength, decisive energy) and from his popular role in easing Mangal Dosha, not from being a planetary deity himself.

Why is Tuesday specifically linked to Hanuman rather than another day?

Tuesday (Mangalvar) is ruled by Mars, the planet of courage, willpower, and combat. Hanuman is traditionally seen as the supreme embodiment of physical strength and disciplined courage, so popular practice pairs him with the day whose ruling planet shares those same qualities. Some traditions also associate his birth with a Tuesday, though this varies by region and calendar.

Do all traditions worship Hanuman on Tuesday?

Tuesday is the dominant popular practice, but it isn't universal. Many devotees also observe Saturday as a Hanuman day, particularly where his worship overlaps with easing Saturn's (Shani's) harsher effects. Regional and sectarian variation is real here, so it's best understood as common custom rather than a fixed rule.

What do people typically do on a Tuesday for Hanuman?

Common practices include reciting the Hanuman Chalisa, visiting a Hanuman temple, offering sindoor and oil, wearing red, and sometimes observing a partial or full fast. Some also consider Tuesday an auspicious day to begin undertakings that call for courage, seeking Hanuman's blessing before starting.
