Simple Daily Astrological Remedies for Beginners

By Astrologer Ananya 9 min read Vedic Astrology · Beginner

You do not need an expensive gemstone or an elaborate ritual to begin working with Vedic remedies. The classical tradition of upaya is full of small, safe, low-cost daily practices — offering water to the Sun, feeding an animal, watering a plant, keeping your word — and most of them are really about building discipline, gratitude, and good conduct into ordinary life.

"Remedy" can sound dramatic, as if something is broken and needs fixing. In Vedic astrology the word used is upaya — simply a supportive measure, a way of aligning yourself with the qualities a planet represents. The gemstones, yantras, and long rituals that get the most attention are only one branch of that tradition, and they should be chosen for a specific chart. The other branch is open to everyone from day one: humble daily acts that ask for time and sincerity rather than money. This guide gathers the gentlest of those practices for beginners.

Why Start with the Simple Practices

Classical texts group upaya into several families — mantra (sound), daan (charity), seva (service), fasting, worship, and, yes, gemstones. Notice how few of those require spending anything. A beginner who offers water to the rising Sun, shares a handful of grain with birds, and treats their parents with patience is already practising three recognised remedies at once. The point is not to buy your way to a better fate but to cultivate the very qualities — steadiness, generosity, respect — that a well-placed planet is said to bestow.

Everything below is safe, inexpensive, and non-committal. None of it replaces medical, legal, or financial advice, and none of it promises a guaranteed outcome. Think of these as good habits with a spiritual frame — useful whether or not you ever open a birth chart.

Morning Remedies: Starting the Day Aligned

The oldest and simplest remedy of all is the daily offering of water to the Sun, known as Surya Arghya. Shortly after sunrise, face east, and slowly pour water from a vessel — a copper pot is traditional — so that the morning light passes through the falling stream. As you pour, many people quietly repeat a Sun mantra such as Om Suryaya Namah. The Sun in Vedic thought (see our guide to the nine planets) governs vitality, confidence, and the sense of self, so greeting it first thing is a small daily vote for your own clarity and discipline.

If a formal offering feels like too much to begin with, start smaller. A minute of stillness, a short mantra, or an honest moment of gratitude before you reach for your phone is itself a remedy in spirit. The tradition values consistency far above elaborateness — a plain practice done every day outweighs a grand one done twice.

Feeding and Charity: The Gentlest Upaya

Sharing food is among the most widely recommended remedies in the whole tradition, and it costs almost nothing. Feeding animals and birds — scattering grain for sparrows and pigeons, keeping water out in summer, offering a chapati to a street dog, feeding a cow where you can — is considered a broad, benevolent act that needs no chart to justify it. Certain associations are especially common: crows are linked with Saturn and the ancestors, dogs also with Saturn (and with Ketu in some readings), and cows are held sacred and connected with nourishing, benefic energy.

Charity, or daan, extends the same idea to people: sharing a meal, giving quietly to someone in need, offering your time. Classical guidance adds two conditions worth remembering — give in the earlier part of the day where you can, and give without expecting anything back. The sincerity of the act matters more than its size.

Nature Remedies: Caring for a Plant

Tending a sacred plant is a quiet, grounding practice. Watering and caring for a Tulsi (holy basil), traditionally kept in many Indian homes, is associated with peace and positive energy in the household. The Peepal (sacred fig) carries a similar reverence; offering water to a Peepal tree, particularly linked in folk practice with relief for Saturn-related difficulty, is a common gentle remedy. Beyond any planetary association, the act of caring for a living thing day after day builds exactly the patience and responsibility these remedies are meant to nurture.

Conduct Remedies: The Ones That Matter Most

The most powerful daily remedies in the Parashari tradition are also the least glamorous: how you behave. Respecting your parents and elders is tied to the Sun (father), the Moon (mother), Jupiter (teachers and elders), and Saturn (duty and service) all at once — which is why classical astrologers so often prescribe it. Honesty, keeping your word, controlling anger, and treating others fairly are considered genuine strengtheners of character and, by extension, of the planets that govern those virtues.

Remedies are supportive habits, not magic. Think of upaya as watering a plant, not flipping a switch. These practices are meant to build the right qualities in you — discipline, gratitude, generosity, honesty — and to keep you aligned with a planet's better nature. They are not superstition to be feared, nor a guaranteed spell to bend fate. And because a truly targeted remedy depends on which planets are strong or afflicted in your chart, anything chart-specific — a fast for a particular planet, a gemstone, a dedicated mantra — is best confirmed with a qualified astrologer rather than copied from a list.

A Weekday Guide to Simple Remedies

Each day of the week (Vara in the Panchang) is ruled by a planet, and matching a small act of worship or charity to the day is an easy way to give your practice a gentle rhythm. The table below pairs each weekday with its ruling planet, a commonly associated deity, and a beginner-friendly act. Treat it as a menu, not an obligation — pick what fits your life.

Notice how Hanuman appears on both Tuesday and Saturday — devotion to him is the traditional bridge across the two most demanding planets, Mars and Saturn. If you remember only one weekday habit, feeding animals or people on Saturday and offering water to the Sun on Sunday are as safe and universally recommended as remedies come.

How to Actually Begin

Do not try to adopt everything at once. Choose one practice — the morning water offering, or feeding birds on your way out — and keep it for a few weeks until it feels natural. Add a second only when the first is a habit. Consistency and sincerity are the whole method; an elaborate routine abandoned in a week helps no one, while a single humble act repeated daily quietly reshapes how you move through the world.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need my birth chart to do these?

No. Every practice in this guide — offering water to the Sun, feeding animals, watering a plant, honest conduct, small weekday charity — is safe and beneficial for anyone, chart or no chart. You only need a chart when you want to target a specific planet that is weak or afflicted in your horoscope, and that is best done with an astrologer.

Do simple remedies really help, or is it just superstition?

Their real work is building the qualities each planet represents — discipline, gratitude, generosity, honesty — through daily repetition. Understood that way, they are supportive habits rather than magic spells. The tradition never promises a guaranteed result, and neither should anyone else; treat any claim of certain outcomes with healthy caution.

Are gemstones necessary to start?

Not at all. Gemstones are a powerful but chart-specific remedy that should be recommended for your particular planetary positions — the wrong stone can be counter-productive. Beginners are far better served by the free, low-risk practices here. If you later want to explore stones, read our guide to the gemstone for each planet and confirm the choice with a qualified astrologer.

What time of day should I do them?

Morning is traditionally favoured — the Sun offering happens just after sunrise, and charity is considered most fitting before noon. That said, sincerity matters more than the clock. A remedy done a little late is far better than one skipped.

When you want a remedy meant for you

The practices here are safe for everyone, but a remedy chosen for your own chart — the right planet, the right day, the right measure — is a more personal thing.
