Which Colour to Wear Each Day According to Astrology

By Astrologer Ananya 9 min read Vedic Astrology · Beginner

Grandmothers who insisted on a yellow dupatta on Thursday, or a friend who never wears black on Sunday, aren't following a random superstition — they're following one of the oldest and most accessible remedies in the Vedic system: dressing by the day's ruling planet.

Of all the astrological practices people pick up without ever reading a birth chart, day-wise colour is probably the most common. It requires no consultation, no calculation, and no expense — just an awareness of which planet governs which day, and a small, conscious choice about what you wear. This guide explains where the practice comes from, gives you the traditional colour table, and — just as important — explains where a generic list like this one reaches its limit.

Why Weekdays Are Linked to Planets

In Vedic astrology, each day of the week (vaar) is ruled by one of the seven classical, visible-to-the-naked-eye grahas: the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and Saturn. This is the same seven-planet sequence that gives the English weekday names their Latin and Norse roots — Sunday for the Sun, Monday for the Moon, Saturday for Saturn, and so on — a reminder that day-planet associations aren't unique to Indian tradition, even if Vedic astrology develops the idea much further.

Each of these seven planets is, in turn, associated with a colour (or a small set of colours) based on its classical nature. The Sun's fire-and-authority quality is read as orange, red, or copper. The Moon's calm, reflective quality is read as white. Mars's assertive, combative quality is read as red. This isn't decorative — the same planet-colour associations show up in gemstone tradition, in Rudraksha Mukhi symbolism, and in ritual offerings, not just in what you wear.

The underlying logic is simple: each day is thought to carry a slightly stronger current of its ruling planet's energy. Wearing that planet's colour is a small, conscious way of aligning yourself with the day rather than working against it — not a guarantee of anything, but a gentle nudge, the same spirit behind simple daily astrological remedies for beginners.

A few practical notes on this table. First, these colours are meant to be worn in support of the day's planet, not to placate a "bad" one — Saturday's black or navy is about invoking Saturn's discipline and protection, not about fear of Shani. Second, most traditions treat these as favourable, complementary colours rather than exclusive ones; wearing something else on a Tuesday isn't treated as a mistake, it just means you skip the small extra alignment.

Where the Generic List Reaches Its Limit

This table works from the day alone, which means it's identical for everyone regardless of their birth chart. That's exactly its appeal — no consultation needed — but it's also its limitation. Your own chart may show a planet that's weak, afflicted, or particularly important for your current dasha period (see our guide to Vimshottari Dasha), and an astrologer reading your chart specifically might suggest leaning into — or in some cases moderating — a particular planet's colour more deliberately than the generic calendar does.

For example, someone going through a demanding Saturn period might be advised to wear more blue and black through the week, not just on Saturdays, to lean into Saturn's steadying qualities during that phase. Someone with a well-placed, strong Jupiter might not need much extra yellow at all. This is the same principle behind personalised gemstone selection, covered in our guide to gemstones for each planet — the generic list is a starting point, your chart is the fine print.

Keep this light. Colour-by-day is one of the gentlest remedies in the entire tradition — low-cost, low-risk, and easy to try. Don't let it become a source of anxiety about "getting it wrong." A coloured accessory, a pocket square, or even a coloured pen on your desk carries the same intent as a full outfit. If a particular colour genuinely doesn't suit your workplace, your wardrobe, or your taste, skipping it on a given day is not going to undo anything else you're doing right.

Simple Ways to Apply This Without Overhauling Your Wardrobe

You don't need seven planet-coded outfits to make use of this. Most people apply it through small, deliberate touches: a tie or dupatta in the day's colour, a stone or bracelet thread, a notebook cover, or even the colour of flowers offered during a short morning prayer. The intent behind the choice matters more than the size of the gesture — a five-second conscious decision each morning is enough to count.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I wear the "wrong" colour on a given day?

Nothing dramatic. This is a supportive practice, not a rule with penalties. Traditional guidance says wearing a day's ruling colour helps align you with that planet's energy — skipping it simply means you skip that small nudge, not that you invite bad luck.

Should I follow the generic day-colour table or my own chart?

The generic table is a reasonable default for anyone without a chart reading handy. But it's a weekday-based system, not a person-based one — it doesn't know which planets are weak or strong for you specifically. An astrologer can tell you which colours support your personal chart, which may differ from the calendar default on certain days.

Can I just wear a coloured accessory instead of a full outfit?

Yes, and this is how most practitioners actually do it. A pocket square, a stone, a bracelet thread, or even a coloured pen is considered sufficient by most traditions — the idea is conscious association with the planet, not a strict dress code.

Is this the same as gemstone remedies?

No. Colour-by-day is a general, low-commitment practice anyone can follow without a chart reading. Gemstone remedies are chart-specific prescriptions meant to be worn continuously, and wearing the wrong one is treated far more seriously in that tradition. Don't confuse a Tuesday red shirt with a prescribed Red Coral.

Want colour guidance tailored to your own chart?

The weekday table is a good starting point, but your chart may call for a more specific approach — leaning into a planet that needs strengthening, or easing off one that's already dominant.
